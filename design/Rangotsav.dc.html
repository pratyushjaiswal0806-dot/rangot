ABOUT ── ಪರಿಚಯ ── परिचय

Ancient art, meet creative code.

Swagat! Rangotsav means “festival of colours”. For centuries, people across India have drawn rangolis at the doorstep, woven kolams before sunrise, carved wooden blocks for cloth and painted lorries till they sing.

Here, each of those crafts becomes a little toy. Pick a pigment, make a mess, save it to your phone. Code is our brush, and like any brush it takes practice.

Behind the Code

RECIPES ── ಪಾಕವಿಧಾನ ── नुस्ख़े

No magic here, only maths and patience. This is how each toy works, written down like Ammamma’s recipe cards.

Recipe Nº 1Rangoli

Mirror, mirror, ×24

Every point you touch is turned by 360°/n around the centre, then flipped across the axis. One stroke, 2n copies, zero effort.

for k in 0…n:
  θ = k · 2π / n
  draw(rotate(p, θ))
  draw(rotate(flipY(p), θ))
Recipe Nº 2Kolam

One line, never lifted

We find a short tour through your dots, then sweep an arc around each one. Leaving a dot, the line crosses over to its neighbour. That crossing is the weave.

tour = nearest(dots).untangle()
for dot in tour:
  arc(dot, from: prev + 0.6,
           to:   next + 0.6)
Recipe Nº 3Lettering

Letters with attitude

Each letter is its own span with a tiny lift and twist. Two stacked text-shadows, nudged a few pixels, fake a misregistered screen print.

span { rotate: 2deg; }
text-shadow:
  6px 5px 0 vermilion,
  10px 9px 0 ink;
Recipe Nº 4Block print

Hand-pressed, by code

The block is drawn once, then we punch hundreds of tiny random holes out of it and multiply it onto the cloth. So no two stamps match.

block.speckle(random)
cloth.blend = 'multiply'
cloth.stamp(block, tilt ±3°)
